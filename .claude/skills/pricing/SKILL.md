# Pricing

## Gather
Product and customer segment, costs (unit cost, delivery time, fees), current price and sales data, competitors and substitutes, goal (margin, volume, positioning), and market currency.

## Method
1. **Floor**: cost plus minimum acceptable margin.
2. **Ceiling**: value to the customer (money saved or earned, time saved, alternative cost).
3. **Market range**: what comparable options charge, using current sources (search before citing competitor prices).
4. **Model**: one-time, subscription, usage-based, per-seat, or hybrid. Pick the one that scales with the value the customer receives.
5. **Packaging**: 3 tiers is a common default. Make the middle tier the target, differentiate tiers by value metric (not by arbitrary limits), and include a clear entry option.
6. **Anchors and framing**: show annual versus monthly, highlight the recommended plan, keep the price list simple.
7. **Test**: A/B or time-boxed price tests, talk to customers, track conversion, churn, average revenue and margin.

## Output
- Recommended price points with the reasoning and the assumptions behind them
- Tier table (name, price, who it is for, what is included)
- Risks, what to test first, and a plan for communicating changes to existing customers (grandfathering, notice period)

State uncertainty plainly. Recommendations are estimates, not guaranteed outcomes, and the decision stays with the user.
